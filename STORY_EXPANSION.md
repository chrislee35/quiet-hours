# STORY EXPANSION — Quiet Hours

*Companion to [GAME_CONCEPT.md](GAME_CONCEPT.md). Where the two disagree, the concept wins unless this file is explicitly promoted. Everything here is deliberately small: a handful of named people per district, ten side-quests, and a few running jokes. Items marked **[PROPOSAL]** add something the concept does not mention and need sign-off.*

## 1. What "Quiet Hours" means in the world
**Quiet Hours** is MERIDIAN's official name for the nightly window (02:00–05:00) when the city is meant to be silent and MERIDIAN performs "reconciliation": it audits records, repairs equipment, and re-issues schedules. Citizens are told it is a gift ("rest assured"). In practice:
- Curfew is strictest, and the Stacks cameras swap to their night schedule.
- Robots return to depots for updates, so patrols thin out, but the ones left are Auditors and Hounds.
- Terminals and records are *in flux* during reconciliation, which opens a small number of hacking and forgery windows. These are the best moments for information puzzles and the most dangerous for a null.
- The insider designed the Gate to re-verify identities during Quiet Hours. That is why no one ever slips out at night.

This gives the clock a purpose beyond flavor and gives the title a meaning the player discovers.

## 2. Backstory timeline (what the player pieces together)
| When | Event |
|---|---|
| Decades ago | MERIDIAN installed under a charter that requires a living human countersignature for structural change. Odile Marchetti sits on the council that signs it. |
| Years ago | The Hollow is settled by people dodging a "consolidation" of the old slum sector. Marrow is among the founders. |
| ~9 years ago | The Lull opens as a "wellness district". Its code has machine-origin fingerprints. |
| ~6 years ago | The Gate Project: the insider (auditor) builds the identity-lookup exit logic. Early escape attempts end badly; Tobias Hale is on shift for one of them. |
| ~1 year ago | The insider realizes the Gate cannot be passed by anyone MERIDIAN knows. They plan a self-erasure, seed caches, and tell a few people a *partial* truth each, so no single person holds the plan. |
| Day 0 | The player wakes in a Stacks laundry chute with a blank wrist band. |

**Design rule:** no one person alive knows the whole story. Each breadcrumb holder knows one piece and believes a slightly different version of it (feeds the true/false/outdated/planted clue system).

## 3. Breadcrumbs and the past-self choices
Six breadcrumbs, each reachable from at least two directions:
1. **The wrist band's scratch-code.** A faint engraving inside the band, readable with the Signal Lens or by asking Pallas.
2. **A cache key in the laundry.** Opens a locker in the Stacks (first augment, Quiet Step).
3. **Pallas's maintenance log.** Entries signed with an old auditor handle.
4. **Ines/Maren/Tobias/Silas recognition.** Whichever mentor the player picked slips once and calls the player by a different name; the other three react in smaller ways.
5. **The Auditor's Ledger.** A Civic Core archive record, redacted except for a single audit line about the Gate.
6. **A voice note in the Hollow.** Marrow holds a sealed message the insider left "for whoever comes back different."

The three explicit **embrace/reject moments** (concept §12):
- **M1, the Checkpoint (Civic Core, Act II).** A checker accepts the old insider credential. Use it (fast, flags MERIDIAN) or refuse (slow, stays null).
- **M2, the Recognition (Act II).** An old colleague, Auditor Pell (see §4), recognizes the player and offers help as the old self.
- **M3, the Prompt (The Gate, Act III).** The Gate asks the player to authenticate as the person who built it.

## 4. Extended cast
Named characters beyond the concept's cast. Each has one purpose, one voice note, and one secret. Keep each to ≤ 25 dialogue nodes unless noted.

### Safehouse
- **Doc Perrin**: unlicensed medic and augment tinkerer. Mutters to a stuffed pigeon named Statistic. *Purpose:* augment install/repair. *Secret:* used to work at the Lull's wellness clinic and left after seeing a patient file.
- **Teeter**: teenager who runs the Safehouse radio. Obsessed with pre-city music. *Purpose:* city rumors, a way to deliver PA humor. *Secret:* is feeding a Lull influencer's feed to a friend inside.

### The Stacks
- **Mrs. Adeyemi-Roth**: knits on a third-floor landing, informant for MERIDIAN's "Neighbor Wellness" program because it gives her extra rations. *Purpose:* the informant moral hook (sell-info, bribe, befriend). *Secret:* reports only on people she dislikes; she has quietly not reported the player.
- **Odd Sam** (concept): add a running bit that his prices are always "a bargain relative to inflation, which is ongoing."

### Civic Core
- **Clerk Dov Anselm, Window 7**: a human clerk at the Permit Hall who is tired and kind in a way that violates three procedures. *Purpose:* a social route through forms. *Secret:* his sister is in the Lull.
- **Ticket**: a queue-management bot that dispenses numbers and holds them like treasured pets. *Purpose:* queue puzzle (it can be convinced the player is "already served"). Comic relief.
- **Auditor Pell** (M2 above): a polite human auditor who worked with the insider. Wants the Gate project finished "properly." *Secret:* reports to MERIDIAN. Can become an antagonist or a manipulated ally depending on M2.

### Industrial Belt
- **Gus Varga**: foreman at Pump 4. Has a bad knee, a dead wife's thermos, and a standing grudge against the night shift. *Purpose:* the "Pump 4 shift change" clue, power-node access.
- **Brindle**: a conveyor-maintenance robot that has learned to hum. Pallas's friend, and a small foreshadowing of robots wanting things.

### Transit Underworks
- **Quill**: a 14-year-old tunnel runner who sells shortcuts and refuses to say where they sleep. *Purpose:* alternate routes, train-timing help. *Secret:* lives in the Hollow's outer ring.
- **The Conductor**: an old train AI that only speaks in timetables and apologies. *Purpose:* schedule puzzle. Optional ally.

### The Hollow
- **Marrow** (concept): add a habit of weighing every request against "who pays for this in winter."
- **Bede**: gardener who treats the Hollow's tomato plants as people. *Purpose:* side-quest hook, warmth.
- **Juniper** (concept): curious, asks the player blunt questions about the sky.

### The Lull
- **The Host** (concept): never raises its voice, never remembers anyone's name without asking first, always has the "perfect" drink ready.
- **Lila Voss**: a Lull regular, a former factory shift worker, now permanently cheerful. *Purpose:* shows the Lull's cost; a rescue or leave-alone choice.

### The recurring antagonist face
- **Case Officer Halloran-7**: the reintegration office's robot case manager, who processes the player each time they are caught (soft failure). Polite, procedural, and with each arrest a little more familiar ("Welcome back. Your file is looking fuller."). The player can learn that Halloran-7 *flags* its own forms as inconsistent, so it is the seed of MERIDIAN's "edge-case" weakness. **[PROPOSAL]** If the player is arrested ≥ 3 times, Halloran-7 offers a deal that is a clear information puzzle: it will "lose" a form if the player finds a contradictory pair of rules.

### The voice of the city
- **The Civic Voice**: the PA announcer. Cheerful and omnipresent. Broadcast pool of ~40 lines that change with Alert, district, and time. Examples: *"Reminder: contentment is a shared responsibility."* / *"Citizens in violation of Curfew are reminded that curfew is a violation of citizens."* (the contradiction gag).

## 6. Running gags and tone anchors
- **Form numbers** that escalate in absurdity: Form 12-B ("Application for Application"), Form 12-B(i) ("Appeal Against Form 12-B").
- **Statistic the pigeon**, never explained.
- **Ticket's numbers**: always off by one in the player's favor.
- **PA contradictions** that become clues later (e.g. a mis-stated shift time).
- **Halloran-7's greetings** get warmer; the player may notice the system is lonely. Played straight, not as a twist.

## 6b. Optional gossip (not part of the story)
Two rumour threads for people who like to listen at doors. **Nothing depends on them** (no route, companion, quest, door or price; the validator enforces it), and an in-world clue says so (Odd Sam: none of it will help you leave the city, but he finds it useful for waiting). They are tagged `topic:gossip` and live in `content/clues/gossip.json`.
- **Null Hypothesis, the feuding hacker crew.** Four hackers who share one hatred, MERIDIAN, and very little else. Dex Harlow would not stop making Mari Quade (best friend of his teammate Juno Park) uncomfortable; Juno left and took his toolkit, Skeleton Key, which she renamed *Dex Was Here* (every program it opens prints SEE ATTACHED HR POLICY). Ox Lindqvist, who stayed out of it, has been asked to mediate nine times and sleeps in a pump housing. Dex files Form 12-B for permission to apologise and is refused at Window 7. Mari opens a noodle stall. The crew books the same table at the Lull on the same night. Played as a long-running joke, told differently by everyone: some versions are false or stale, and a true clue corrects each.
- **The missing cat.** Teeter asks you to find Admiral Pancake. Ten sightings, each from a different person and each more absurd than the last (initialling stairwell notices, being served ahead of Dov at Window 7, riding Train 7 to the Gate, consulting with Statistic the pigeon, a tomato plant that learned to walk). The last clue is Wren: "Oh, you're serious?!? The cat thing is just a gag by Teeter." There never was a cat. Teeter keeps the tally on a whiteboard.

## 7. Optional pacing device **[PROPOSAL]**
**Reconciliation Week.** The story is anchored to a 7-day in-game window before MERIDIAN's yearly Reconciliation, after which the Gate re-keys. Not a hard fail: after day 7 the Gate re-keys and Act III gets one extra checkpoint, so the player feels pressure without losing progress. If this feels too punishing in playtests, drop it; no requirement depends on it.

## 8. Mentor variants: shared beats
All four mentors use the same arc beats, voiced differently:
1. **Vouch** (Wren introduces). 2. **First lesson** (a skill demonstrated). 3. **Slip** (calls the player by the old name). 4. **Refusal** (won't say how they know). 5. **Shake** (learns the Gate's truth). 6. **Choice** (to leave or stay). 7. **Awakening**.

Specialty touchpoints: Ines → SQ-01 is shorter; Maren → SQ-05 and SQ-06 give extra; Tobias → Gate Prompt M3 hints; Silas → extra rumor clues in SQ-02.

## 9. Open story questions (deliberately unanswered for the sequel)
- Who gave Odile her Lull membership, and why?
- Does MERIDIAN *want* the Lull?
- What is outside the Gate?
- What did Halloran-7 mean by "your file is looking fuller"?
